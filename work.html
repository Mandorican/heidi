Lockheed Martin — Website Concept

The problem: Lockheed Martin's 2023 redesign leans minimal — white space, a boxed layout, a compact video hero. Clean, but when I think of Lockheed Martin, white isn't the color that comes to mind.

My approach: I concepted an alternative homepage built around black and Lockheed Martin's signature blue — colors closer to what the brand already embodies: power and sophistication, security and reliability. A full-width, dark-dominant hero anchored by the SR-71 Blackbird replaces the boxed video, chosen deliberately over an in-production aircraft for a first impression meant to make visitors pause.

The outcome: A concept exploring how color and hero treatment could reinforce brand identity more directly — background imagery throughout acts as visual dividers between sections, keeping the scroll cohesive. Done for non-commercial, educational purposes; all product imagery and brand assets belong to their respective owners.

Zion Zeta — Logo + Brand Identity

The problem: As a personal project for my own content-creator persona, I needed an on-screen visual identity that could hold up consistently across streams, social platforms, and merch — not just a logo.

My approach: I built a full identity system around a high-contrast black, crimson, and silver palette, a laurel-wreathed monogram, and a Quasimoda/Lato Bold typography pairing — designed so the mark inverts cleanly across all four color combinations.

The outcome: A bold, angular wordmark that reads the same whether it's on a business card, a t-shirt, or a stream overlay — giving the persona one consistent visual identity across every surface it appears on.

Spirit of Love — Brand Identity + UI Design

The problem: A small business owner wanted to launch an online shop for spiritually meaningful silver pendant jewelry, but was DIYing it on Wix with limited technical background and no cohesive brand to build around.

My approach: I handled brand identity and logo design end-to-end, then rebuilt the site in WordPress after consultation surfaced Wix's limitations — defining target audience, goals, and content structure from scratch. At her request, I migrated everything back to Wix in the final stretch so she could manage it herself without touching code.

The outcome: A cohesive brand and a site she could confidently share and maintain independently, over a 6-month engagement. As a companion exercise, I also built a high-fidelity mobile prototype in Figma — skipping low-fidelity wireframes to focus on learning the tool, a scoping decision that taught me high-fidelity work often takes longer, not less.

Heidi Colón — Portfolio Website

The problem: I needed a place to showcase my design work without paying over a hundred dollars a year for hosting, so I set out to build something on GitHub Pages that I could host for a smaller fee per year.

My approach: What started as a single landing page with a row of reference-point buttons grew into a full multi-page site — Home, Skills, and Work — with a light and dark theme system, a dropdown navigation menu, and small interactive details throughout, all built and hosted for a fraction of what a paid platform would cost.

The outcome: A live, working portfolio site that doubles as a design sample in its own right — proof I can take a project from a scoped-down starting point to a polished, functioning product without a big hosting budget behind it.
